abstract: CRISPR/Cas-9 is a novel technology that functions in microbial systems by identifying degraded DNA.  Recently, advances in genetic engineering has allowed scientists to use CRISPR-Cas9-based elements as a tool to modify specific strands of DNA in a multitude of living systems, therefore increasing its popularity and usage. However, it is very expensive to synthesize RNA sequences for CRISPR experiences. Buying singular bases and assembling them also requires more time and effort. Given the emerging need for inexpensive and accurate RNA sequences, our team has created an algorithm that uses constraint satisfaction and parallel computing. Utilizing a cost and hamming distance function, the algorithm outputs degenerate sequences.
